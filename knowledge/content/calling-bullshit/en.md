The book grew out of a course the two authors teach at the University of
Washington and is written for everyone who meets numbers, studies and charts
every day. It explains how claims made with data come across as convincing
although they are false, for instance through a biased sample, a correlation
sold as a cause, or a chart whose axis starts at the wrong place. Instead of
formulas it offers questions you can ask of any number.

## Why it fits here

One chapter of the book deals with data visualisation, and there stands the
principle of proportional ink, on which Claus O. Wilke also relies in
[[fundamentals-of-data-visualization]]: where an area shows a value, it must
be proportional to that value. The lesson [[misleading-charts]] builds on it
and shows the tricks in ggplot2.

## Editions

The book came out in 2020 from Random House in New York, in English. The
authors' website holds the course material, videos and tools, among them a
page on proportional ink.

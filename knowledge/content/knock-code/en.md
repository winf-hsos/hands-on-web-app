## What this is about

In this experiment you and a partner build your own code and use it to
transmit words without speaking. You have only two signals: **knock** once on
the table with your knuckle (K) and **clap** your hands once (C). By the end
you will know from your own measurements what code words of equal and of
different lengths cost.

## What you need

- two people sitting back to back, so that they cannot see each other
- paper and pen for each person
- a stopwatch, for example on a phone

## The characters

You transmit only eight characters: **E, N, I, S, R, A, T** and the **space**.
They make a surprising number of words, for example *STAR*, *RAIN*, *TEN* or
*NEAT*.

## Round 1: code words of equal length

1. Work out together how many signals a code word needs at least so that eight
   characters fit.
2. Write a table in which every character gets exactly that many signals.
   **Both** of you need a copy, and it has to be the same one.
3. The sender picks three words without revealing them and knocks them one
   after the other. The receiver writes them down and times the transmission.
4. Enter the time and the number of wrongly received characters in the
   measurement table.

## Round 2: code words of different lengths

1. Now give the frequent characters (E, N, I) shorter code words and the rare
   ones longer code words. Make sure no code word is the beginning of another,
   otherwise the receiver cannot tell where a character ends.
2. Transmit three new words in the same way and measure again.

> [!TIP]
> If you are not sure whether your table is unambiguous, write all code words
> one below the other and check for every pair whether the shorter one is the
> beginning of the longer one.

## Round 3 (optional): a secret change

Without telling anyone, the sender swaps the code words of two characters in
their table and transmits a word. Does an error message appear, or does a
different word simply arrive?

## Evaluation

- Which round was faster on average, measured in signals and in seconds? Do
  both answers agree?
- In which round were there more errors, and why?
- Is there a word that gets longer in round 2 than in round 1? What do its
  letters have in common?
- What arrived in round 3, and why did nobody notice an error?

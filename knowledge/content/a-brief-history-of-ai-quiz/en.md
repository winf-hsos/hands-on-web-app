## An agent builds a quiz app on the history of AI with the instruction to use only the knowledge base it created before. What is the main purpose of this instruction? {#bh-001}

- [x] Every question can be traced back to a checked entry with a source, instead of to what the model believes it knows.
  > Correct: the knowledge base was checked against sources; the model's general knowledge was not.
- [ ] The app runs faster, because it does not need a connection to the internet.
  > The instruction has nothing to do with the app's speed; it concerns where the content comes from.
- [ ] The model can no longer make mistakes, because the knowledge base is correct.
  > The instruction lowers the risk but does not rule out mistakes: the model can misrepresent a good source too.
- [ ] Copyright is respected, because the knowledge base contains only facts.
  > That may hold on the side, but it is not the purpose of the instruction. The point is that every statement stays checkable.

## An agent's timeline dates a milestone to 1957 and links a source. What is the most reliable way to check this? {#bh-002}

- [ ] Ask the agent whether it is sure about the year.
  > The agent's answer is again generated text and not an independent check.
- [x] Open the linked source and see whether it gives the same year for the same event.
  > Correct: trust, but check means comparing the statement with the source it relies on.
- [ ] Ask a second chatbot for the year and accept it if both agree.
  > Two models can make the same mistake, for example because it is in their training data. Agreement does not replace a source.
- [ ] Check whether the year looks plausible compared with the neighbouring milestones.
  > Plausibility catches gross errors but not one that is off by a year. That needs the source.

## In "The Bitter Lesson" (2019) Rich Sutton draws a conclusion from seventy years of AI research. Which? {#bh-003}

- [x] General methods that get better with more computation, such as learning and search, have in the long run overtaken approaches built on human knowledge.
  > Correct: this is the bitter lesson, because researchers kept wanting to build in their own knowledge and lost out in the long run.
- [ ] Systems with rules written by experts remain more reliable than learned systems and will replace them.
  > Sutton reaches the opposite conclusion: hand-written knowledge helped in the short run and was overtaken in the long run.
- [ ] Progress in AI came mainly from better programming languages and development tools.
  > Software development tools are not Sutton's subject. He compares knowledge that humans build in with computation.
- [ ] AI research has failed repeatedly because computers will never have enough memory for general intelligence.
  > Sutton argues precisely that growing computation has been the driver of progress.

## What did Alan Turing propose in "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" in 1950? {#bh-004}

- [x] to replace the question "Can machines think?" with a test in which a machine has to pass as a human in a written conversation
  > Correct: Turing called it the imitation game; today it is known as the Turing test.
- [ ] the first artificial neural network, a model of a neuron that learns from examples
  > Learning neuron models came later, for example Rosenblatt's perceptron of 1958.
- [ ] the term "artificial intelligence" for a summer workshop at Dartmouth
  > The term comes from the 1955 proposal for the Dartmouth workshop held the following year, not from Turing.
- [ ] a program that beats the world chess champion
  > That only happened in 1997, with Deep Blue against Garry Kasparov.

## Why is 1956 often named as the year AI was born? {#bh-005}

- [x] At a workshop at Dartmouth College the field of research got its name, "artificial intelligence".
  > Correct: the proposal for the workshop coined the term, and with the meeting AI begins as a field of its own.
- [ ] In that year the first computer was sold commercially, which made AI research possible in the first place.
  > Commercial computers existed from the early 1950s; this has nothing to do with the name of the field.
- [ ] Turing published the test named after him.
  > Turing's paper appeared in 1950, six years earlier.
- [ ] A neural network was trained on a computer for the first time.
  > The perceptron was presented in 1958; 1956 stands for the name and the founding of the field.

## An agent is asked to publish an app on the history of AI that it built itself. Its folder also contains the downloaded sources. What does not belong in the publication? {#bh-006}

- [x] the full texts of the downloaded sources
  > Correct: the rights to the texts belong to publishers and authors; what gets published is the link, not the copy.
- [x] the API key used to generate the narrator's voice
  > Correct: a published key can be used by anyone, at the account holder's expense.
- [ ] the knowledge base with the facts and the links to the sources
  > It may be included and makes the app checkable: anyone can follow the path from the entry to the source.
- [x] the photo of the pinboard with the students' handwriting
  > Correct: handwriting is personal data; without consent it does not belong on the web.
- [ ] the app's code
  > The code may be published; it is the work in question.

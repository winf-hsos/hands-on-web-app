The short article in *Science* starts from an observation: many data
analyses that are criticised later do not calculate wrongly but answer a
different question from the one attributed to them. A pattern noticed while
exploring is reported as generally valid, or a good prediction is read as an
explanation. The authors therefore propose assigning every analysis to one of
six types: descriptive, exploratory, inferential, predictive, causal and
mechanistic. A sequence of yes-or-no questions leads to the right type.

## Why it fits here

The lesson [[analytic-questions]] presents this decision tree and applies it
to the survey on milk and chocolate. The article itself is two pages long
and easy to read; at *Science* it is behind a paywall, but usually freely
accessible through a university library.

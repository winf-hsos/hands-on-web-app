## What it is about

Georges Ifrah follows numbers from notches on bones through the number
systems of Babylon, Egypt, Greece, China and the Maya to the Indian digits
with their zero, which came to Europe by way of the Arab world. Along the way
he shows how many bases people have actually used, from twenty in the
counting of the Maya to sixty, which still lives on in our hours and minutes.

## Why it fits here

The lesson [[place-value-systems]] claims that ten is only the number of our
fingers. Ifrah's book is the long version of that claim, with the evidence:
the place value idea was invented more than once, and it took centuries until
the zero made it work. Read it for the story, not for the arithmetic.

## Editions

The book first appeared in French in 1981 and was expanded in 1994. The
English translation used here was published by Wiley in 2000; a German
edition appeared as *Universalgeschichte der Zahlen*.

## What it is about

An experiment assigns chance itself, and that is why you may conclude a cause
from its result. Most of the time, though, you only have data that simply
accumulated, from a survey or a till system, say. This book is about asking
cleanly for causes even then. The first part introduces research design: what
you actually want to know, how to write it down as a causal diagram and which
influences you have to rule out for it. The second part is a toolbox of the
common methods, from regression and matching through difference-in-differences
to instrumental variables and regression discontinuity.

## Why it fits here

In the lesson [[analytic-questions]], causal questions sit low in the
decision tree, because they demand the most. If you want to know how to
answer them with observational data nonetheless, and where such answers
fail, this is the thorough and readable continuation. The examples can be
reproduced in R.

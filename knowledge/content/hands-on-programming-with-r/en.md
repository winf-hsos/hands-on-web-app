The book teaches R as a programming language, not as a tool for one
particular analysis. Each of its three parts is a project: first you build
dice that can be loaded, then a card game, and finally a slot machine. Along
the way you learn what objects in R are made of, how to reach and change
their parts, how R looks up names, how to write your own functions, build
loops and make code faster.

## Why it fits here

If after the path [[getting-started-with-r]] you want to understand what happens beneath
the surface of R, this is the place. For analysing data with dplyr and
ggplot2, the author himself recommends [[r-for-data-science]] next. Learning
through games and simulations also suits projects such as the Monty Hall
problem.

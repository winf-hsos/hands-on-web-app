The first part of the book starts from the data and asks which visualisation
suits them: for amounts, distributions, proportions, associations, time
series, places and uncertainty. The second part collects principles of good
figures, such as that the area of a bar must match its value, how to use
colour, what to do when points overlap, and why three-dimensional charts
hardly ever help. The third part deals with file formats, software and
telling a story with figures.

## Why it fits here

The book contains no code, although all its figures were made with R and
ggplot2. That is exactly why it complements [[r-for-data-science]] well:
there you learn how to build a chart in R, here which chart answers the
question and how to spot a misleading one.
